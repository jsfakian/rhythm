Governance, evidence and a European repository.

UoC activities connect responsible data management with the development, validation and roll-out of the RHYTHM CT dose and image repository.

WP1

Data Management Plan

This task will establish the project’s Data Management Plan and set up and lead the RHYTHM Data Management Board. The DMB will oversee all aspects of data governance, ensuring adherence to high standards of data quality, security and compliance throughout the RHYTHM project.

The work focuses on data interoperability, secure storage, and adherence to EU data-protection and EHDS regulations in alignment with the ELSIB.

WP5

Development of a European repository

A European dose and image repository will be established, enabling dose benchmarking through the concept of DRLs, as well as direct comparisons of scanner protocol settings and image quality for specific clinical indications across institutions using the same scanner model.

  • Create a structured reference database where radiology departments can compare their DRLs against similar institutions.
  • Enable users to compare scanner protocol settings, dose indices and image quality with institutions using the same CT scanner model, fostering standardisation and reducing variability in radiation doses.
WP5 task sequence

Four connected stages.

From agreeing clinical indications to European data collection, secure infrastructure and real-world validation.

5.1

Identification of Clinical Indications

This task will identify priority CT clinical indications for establishing DRLs in paediatric patients, adolescents and young adults. The selection will build on EUCLID and EuroSafe Imaging work and will consider radiation-protection relevance, coverage of different anatomical regions, and clear terminology. The final list will be agreed by expert consensus.

5.2

Data Collection and AI-Enhanced Analysis

A geographically representative network of European hospitals will collect CT protocol parameters, patient-size metrics, dose data and anonymised images for selected clinical indications. The resulting database will reflect variations in clinical practice across Europe. The DMB and ELSIB will support data quality, harmonisation, anonymisation and legal compliance. AI methods will be used to identify anomalies, including unusually high or low radiation doses.

5.3

Development of the Repository

A secure repository will be developed to support data submission, storage, retrieval, search and interactive reporting. Users will be able to analyse data by age group, scanner model, examination type, site and time period, and to compare local results with repository benchmarks. Registration, access control, data-protection measures and governance policies will ensure secure and legally compliant use.

5.4

Repository Validation and Roll-Out

The repository will be tested to confirm that data submission, storage, retrieval, search, filtering, reporting and benchmarking functions operate accurately and securely. Pilot testing with selected radiology departments and demonstrations at scientific conferences will support system refinement, real-world integration, dissemination and long-term sustainability.

Built with governance in the architecture.

Data quality, harmonisation, secure access, data-protection safeguards and accountable governance are integrated across the collection, repository-development and validation tasks—not added afterwards.
